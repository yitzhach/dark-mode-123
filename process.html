Process — No. 03

Process

Five stages

Five stages, in order. The first two are mine to control; the last three belong partly to the material. That division is the whole subject of the work.

Studio — Southern California / Southern Kentucky
01 Material 02 Formwork 03 Cast 04 Cure 05 Finish
01
Material

Nothing arrives pre-coloured.

Portland cement, casting plaster, pigment, and aggregate gathered from [site] — mixed by hand in batches small enough to pour in one pass. Pigment goes in wet, which means it never lands quite where it was put.

Cement · plasterRaw pigmentMixed by hand
02
Formwork

The part I actually control.

Steel cut and welded to size, forms built and sealed by hand. Tolerances are tight here because everything after this stage refuses instruction. Order is front-loaded on purpose.

Welded steelSealed forms±[tolerance]
03
Cast

One pour. No second attempt.

Poured in a single continuous pass, vibrated out by hand. Air finds its own routes through the form. From the moment the pour begins, every decision is permanent.

Single passHand-vibratedNo retouching
04
Cure

Two weeks in the dark, and it keeps moving.

Slow hydration under damp cloth. Hairlines open, pigment migrates toward the edges, and the surface goes on changing after the form comes off. This is the entropy half of the title — and the half I like.

[14] daysDamp cureUnpredictable
05
Finish

Ground back, then left open.

Faces are ground and honed by hand until the aggregate reads, then left unsealed. The piece goes on ageing in whatever room it lives in — slowly, and not to my schedule.

Hand-groundUnsealedStill ageing

Order is what I bring. Entropy is what finishes the work.
